## 8th <abbr title="Philippine National Kendo Tournament">PNKT</abbr>: my 1kyu shinsa & first shiai

On October 24–25, 2026, the 8th Philippine National Kendo Tournament was held at Gameville Ball Park, Mandaluyong. In the morning of the 24th was shinsa, which is the kendo grading exam, and in the afternoon was the shiai or the competition, starting with individuals. 25th was a continuation of the shiai, but this time for team matches.

I initially didn’t plan to take the shinsa, but nawili ako kasi my other batchmates were gonna take the ikkyu exam and also competing 🙈 I figured I guess as a first time, _why not_, ganon.

### Preparing for the shinsa

Nung nag decide na ako to take the exam, I really had to sit with my current skill level nun. That was less than a month before the exam.

I knew I was slowly getting the hang of doing the strikes, and conceptually I understood the things I needed to take note, pero pag pagod ka na after doing it so many times… dun na papasok yung mas magandang muscle memory na lang yung mga moves kesa nag-iisip ka pa. Ideally I wouldn’t have to _think_ about it too much, and for me to achieve that I needed to do it a lot more times. So I needed to increase the number of times I attended <abbr title="kendo practice">keiko</abbr> sessions.

I only had around 2 weeks left to really prepare for the exam, lalo na with the level I was planning to take. That’s when I attended more weekday sessions with [Metroken](https://www.metrokendoclub.com/), and from there I would get pointers from other senpais and senseis on what else I should work on with my kendo.

Yung mga weekday keiko medyo mas intense than the weekend keiko kasi these sessions are led by our head sensei, Wada-sensei. May expectation rin talaga na you have to keep up and do the strikes correctly when going through the drills here. And since generally mas konti attendance here compared sa weekend keiko, mas maraming opportunities rin matapat sa higher level senseis who would either have comments on your stance or your strike, or generally would challenge you to do better or more during jigeiko time.

One misconception I had when I was starting out was that kendo was mostly arm movements. When in reality, it’s the whole body, and more focused on the feet, even. Di ko inakala na feet would play a huge role with a kendo strike! And that really was something I had to fix before my exam. One concept included in a proper kendo strike, according to my senseis, was to have good fumikomi.[^1] Specifically, dapat hindi ka lang nagrereach with your arms, but you’re basically committing your whole body into the strike and moving your whole self forward as you hit. So that was what I tried focusing more during the practices I’d attend.

We also had a mock shinsa seminar on 11 October, so around 2 weeks before the actual shinsa, to get participants to familiarize ourselves with how a shinsa goes, how to enter the exam area and how to exit, things to watch out for, and also as a way to see how we’d fare with our current skillset and what we should improve on if ever.

The outcome of that mock shinsa was: everyone but one person passed 1kyu. One had to be noted as 2kyu. The senseis didn’t really say who it was, but I knew that was me. All my senpais at the time—most of them helping run the whole seminar—would try to remind me to keep my fumikomi in my strikes then. After we got the results, I understood why they said that. To be fair naman, that time was also the time I really just allowed myself to do all the mistakes possible kasi para alam ko what to correct and what I should take note of.

Aside from taking note of the footwork, I also needed to better pace myself and actually do proper breathing exercises as while we were doing the shinsa, I was commended for having good kiai but day after that paos ako 😂 The recommendation is to shout from the diaphragm and not the throat, otherwise mapapaos ka. Well… ayun lmao 🙈

Another thing was pacing myself. I don’t have the same amount of stamina I had in my mid-20’s—diba nga [30 na ‘ko](/blog/turning-30/) lol—so I really had to manage my breathing so I wouldn’t be too tired doing my strikes. This was another thing noted by the senseis as feedback after the mock shinsa: I shouldn’t let it sound like I’m tired. Sobrang obvious nun na ubos na ako, and while maganda nga na malakas sigaw ko, that also backfired sakin kasi rinig na rinig rin sa hinga ko na pagod na ako hahaha

So with that, I took all those learnings—plus the conviction to _not_ settle with 2kyu and really do all I can so I can get 1kyu—into the last few keiko sessions I’d attend before [my trip to Bali](#balirette-with-the-girls).

### While in Bali, get a stick and practice suburi

I didn’t think I’d have a chance to practice kendo while I was in Bali, but thankfully my friend and I went to a gym that had a spot where they had a lot of sticks. One of them seemed long enough to be like a shinai! Though it was way lighter 😂 I just used that to do some [suburi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suburi) so at least di ako “rusty” when I get back to the Philippines.

After doing all these, last is jitsugi. The goal is to get at least 2 ippon in, and it’s around 45 seconds of just continuous striking. We were advised that this is more for checking good and proper form for kendo rather than being like a shiai where you have the extra goal of not making your opponent get a point (so therefore, blocking), and that for jitsugi, even if your opponent gets a point with you, you should also still keep your form with your strike since that will also still be judged by the senseis.

> Something amusing I learned after the fact: my senpais and senseis would all continue to commend my _kiai_, na rinig ako kahit sa kabilang court. 😆 Happy ako dyan kasi feeling ko yan yung sure akong madadala ko sa kendo ko wahaha

Then we wait for the initial results. Nun pa lang actually nakalagay na number ko sa 1kyu column so tuwa na ako 😆 but I couldn’t celebrate fully yet as we had one more thing to do before we can be fully judged and recommended for 1kyu: kata.

We had to do the [bokuto ni yoru kendo kihon waza keiko ho](https://www.kendo-guide.com/bokuto-ni-yoru-kendo-kihon-waza-keiko-ho.html). We just call it the kihon kata, though I think this term is more reserved for the 1dan kata? I’m not really sure… but just to be really clear, this is the basic kata that is for kyu-level takers.

I had no more videos while we were doing kata as Richard had to go home already then, but basically I did all my kata well—except for receiving the first kata where I did the wrong receiving for the first 2 strikes!! 🙈 lowrd I had to raise my hand to ask if I could repeat it and I did it correct na the second time around—and I was paired up with my senpai from Metroken, Irose-senpai. We did our kata correctly, and we both passed ikkyu 😁

### My first shiai

Summary of this experience is really just, more for the experience and less about winning, per se. I did put my all in my matches, though. My main goal was to not let my opponent get a point from me, and the bonus was just if I could get a point in (since I wasn’t that confident yet that I’d cleanly get a strike that would count as ippon with my skillset).

For my individuals matches, I was pooled with Steph from IGAKen and Diane-senpai who I knew as part of the Philippine Kendo team.
